The intent

We wanted a house that sits on the rock as if it had always been there, and leaves the horizon exactly as it found it.

— Design brief, sheet 01

A house built from the mountain it stands on.

Al Shafa House is a concept for a single-family house on the granite of Jabal Daka in the Al Shafa highlands near Taif, about 2,570 meters above sea level, over an open western valley. It has two levels. Below is a stone plinth set into the rock for sleeping and bathing. Above is a glass pavilion for living, cooking and watching the weather come in.

The stone dug out for the plinth becomes its walls. A granite blade 26 meters long anchors the house to the mountainside and carries the roof, which reaches nine meters out over the terrace without a column on the valley side.

Six materials, chosen to weather.

Each sample is the same surface the film uses, lit in real time as the page loads. Nothing is painted, laminated or veneered.

Split-face granite

Quarried from the plinth excavation and dry-laid in long, low courses. Used for the spine, the fin and the hearth.

Vein-cut travertine

Honed and unfilled, so the pores stay open and grip wet feet. Used for the terraces, the pool coping and the gallery wall.

White oak

Wide boards with a hard-wax oil finish. Used for the floor and the soffit, so floor and ceiling read as one material.

Patinated bronze

Left to darken in the mountain air. Used for the roof edge, the mullions and the door pulls.

Wool bouclé

A heavy, undyed weave for the living-room seating.

Saddle leather

Vegetable-tanned, for the lounge chairs. It darkens where hands rest.
